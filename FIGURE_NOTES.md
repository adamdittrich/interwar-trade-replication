# Figure captions, notes, and sources

These captions accompany the vector PDFs in `output/manuscript_assets/figures/` and follow the submitted manuscript.

## Figure 1. An older hierarchy loses fit while recorded link entry remains small

File: `output/manuscript_assets/figures/main_1_established_map.pdf`

**Notes:** The upper panel reports $100P_t$ on positive-total 1927–1929 support, including External. Shading spans the actual minimum and maximum from deleting each named country and all incident dyads; it is a sensitivity range. The lower panel reports the mean world-portfolio share of internal cells recorded as zero throughout 1927–1929 and positive in the evaluation year. Outcomes retain their original denominators.

**Source:** Author's calculations from League of Nations trade statistics (League of Nations 1930, 1937, 1939).

**Alternative text:** Both historical prediction scores decline across the 1930s, while the portfolio share attached to previously zero internal cells remains below one percent.

## Figure 2. Historical allocation retains information beyond distance and borders

File: `output/manuscript_assets/figures/main_2_geography.pdf`

**Notes:** Entries are $100G_t$, the reduction in deviance from using frozen historical affinity instead of frozen distance-and-border coefficients, with current margins refitted in each case. Both predictors use the same established internal support. Shading gives the actual range across single-country deletions. External is excluded.

**Source:** Author's calculations from League of Nations trade statistics (League of Nations 1930, 1937, 1939). Geography inputs are defined in Appendix A3.1.

**Alternative text:** The historical predictor's advantage over geography falls over time but remains positive for export and import declarations, including every country-deletion endpoint estimate.

## Figure 3. An aging pre-crisis map and a recent map tell different stories

File: `output/manuscript_assets/figures/main_3_fixed_horizon.pdf`

**Notes:** Each panel compares rolling predictions with the 1927–1929 anchor. For each rolling training-window length and evaluation year, both predictors and the margins-only benchmark use the intersection of their established supports. Each rolling model predicts the year immediately after its training window. Dashed anchor series therefore differ slightly across the one-year and three-year comparisons. The vertical guide marks the first post-training year, not an estimated break.

**Source:** Author's calculations from League of Nations trade statistics (League of Nations 1930, 1937, 1939).

**Alternative text:** One-year and three-year rolling predictions remain substantially stronger than their same-support 1927–1929 anchors by the late 1930s in both declarations.

## Figure 4. Germany gains regional weight through an uneven trajectory

File: `output/manuscript_assets/figures/main_4_germany.pdf`

**Notes:** Annual Germany–regional interactions from the observed 21-country internal panel, 1928–1937, with directed-pair, origin-year and destination-year effects. The region comprises Bulgaria, Greece, Hungary, Romania and Yugoslavia. The sole reference year is 1933. Bars are conventional 95% pair-cluster intervals. The 1933 reference point is normalized to zero and has no estimated interval.

**Source:** Author's calculations from League of Nations trade statistics (League of Nations 1930, 1937, 1939).

**Alternative text:** Both directions of Germany's regional trade strengthen by 1937; exporter and importer event paths differ in their pre-1934 movements and subsequent timing.

## Figure 5. Movement across the core boundary varies by country

File: `output/manuscript_assets/figures/main_5_external.pdf`

**Notes:** The residual share of world trade outside the fixed 19-country core. It includes Bulgaria and Yugoslavia as well as every non-core country. Export and import panels retain their respective reporter denominators. The country selection illustrates distinct trajectories; complete country series are reported in the appendix.

**Source:** Author's calculations from League of Nations trade statistics (League of Nations 1930, 1937, 1939).

**Alternative text:** France, Germany, the United Kingdom and Czechoslovakia follow different paths in the share of exports and imports assigned to the fixed outside-core aggregate.
